✳ THE DEVELOPMENT DIARY / DAY 5

Beyond the house.

HomePulse at home. Me somewhere else. ♡

The first dashboard was working. Now I needed it to be useful even when I wasn't in the same building.

By now I had a rather nice HomePulse dashboard running on a Raspberry Pi at home.

I could look at the Bluetooth receivers, check the Tapo activity timeline and see which bits of the system were reporting. That was a big improvement on reading logs or getting an AI agent to query the database for me.

But there was a fairly obvious problem.

What use is a home-awareness dashboard if I can only see it when I'm at home?

The whole point of this project is that I want some useful context when I'm somewhere else. Especially when the equipment eventually moves to Mum's.

One ticket. One tidy desk.

So I gave my agent the next job: make HomePulse privately accessible from my phone or laptop, without opening Mum's eventual home network to the public internet.

Then I got on with cleaning my desk.

Really, that's about as exciting as my physical contribution was.

We'd already thought through the architecture. AWS Systems Manager was working for restricted engineering maintenance, but that's not what I wanted for everyday viewing. I didn't want to open an engineering console every time I fancied checking the dashboard.

The chosen approach was Tailscale, a private network between devices I authorise. The agent set up a secure HTTPS route to the existing dashboard, while keeping the Raspberry Pi's other services out of reach over that connection.

No public website exposing the data. No router port forwarding. And no reason for Mum to learn another piece of technology.

Does it really work when I'm away?

Once the setup was complete, I tried it on my Android phone using mobile data with Wi-Fi switched off.

HomePulse opened.

I could see the same genuine test-bench information, now through the private connection.

Then I turned Tailscale off and tried opening the page in a fresh tab. This time it couldn't connect.

That's exactly what I wanted: access when I'm authorised, no accidental public access when I'm not.

I was even thinking it would be rather fun to check it while having a break from skating, just to make the point that I could be doing something completely unrelated to software and still look in on the system. The mobile-data test had already demonstrated remote access; that would be an everyday-life check rather than a claim of a new technical breakthrough.

♡

HomePulse was at home.
I didn't have to be.

It also needed to survive a restart

The agent didn't just make a private link and call the job done.

It checked that the dashboard still worked locally if the private networking service stopped, that the separate engineering maintenance route remained available, and that the connection came back after a real Raspberry Pi reboot.

It also verified that the services behind HomePulse, including the Bluetooth and Tapo collection, were still doing their jobs.

That matters because a remote dashboard is only useful if it tells the truth about how fresh its information is. A camera whose history was last checked an hour ago is not the same as a live notification, and a Pi that's offline can't magically show new readings.

WHAT WE ACTUALLY PROVED Private remote viewing from my phone.

The bench dashboard loaded over Tailscale on Android with mobile data. It was inaccessible in a fresh tab after Tailscale disconnected. The engineering evidence also records recovery after a Pi reboot, restricted network access and 41 passing tests on both the workstation and Pi.

It was still a home bench, not a commissioned system at Mum's house.

Why I care about such a boring-sounding feature

Remote access doesn't have the visual appeal of the dashboard or the novelty of the Bluetooth sensors. But to me it's one of the most useful parts of the project.

I'm Mum's sole carer, and I have a family and a busy job. I want to spend my time being useful to the people who need me, rather than making unnecessary journeys to look at a computer screen.

Once HomePulse is installed and properly tested at Mum's, being able to check its evidence without being physically there should make a real difference.

And it's another reminder of why I use agentic development. I supplied the objective and the architectural constraints, the agent carried out the implementation and tests, and I checked the real-world result on my phone.

All while I'd been tidying my desk.

Now the next job is to make the dashboard useful for setting up the house, not just watching the equipment.
